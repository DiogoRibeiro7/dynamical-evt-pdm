\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study demonstrated a reproducible way to test whether clustered extremes become early warnings, and the answer on the evaluated public data is mostly negative. The registered recurrence score detected the two held-out compressor failures, but only with 2631 and 852 alarm episodes and false-alarm burdens of 105.34 and 51.47 alarms per operating day. These detections are not operationally plausible maintenance signals. Nor is the comparison against simple policies settled by episode counts alone: the engineering threshold raises fewer episodes while keeping the system under warning for most of the MetroPT test period, so alarm burden is only interpretable when interruption frequency and warning exposure are reported together.

The target-region evidence is the most informative positive contrast. The failure-prototype region missed the held-out MetroPT event, but detected the held-out MetroPT2 event with 157 alarms and 9.41 false alarms per day. That divergence shows that recurrence regions can matter, but it also shows that their failure-proximal meaning is not stable across the two compressor datasets. Matched controls further narrow the interpretation: the MetroPT2 prototype result is better than several structure-preserving controls, but not uniformly better than occupancy-matched random regions.

The main failure mechanisms are now separated per method rather than per dataset. Score quality, threshold selection, and alarm conversion can each destroy utility, and the decomposition assigns 31 of 38 method-dataset rows to alarm burden, 4 to event matching, and 3 to the score or threshold layer. In the registered compressor score, threshold exceedances can recover the event, but alarm conversion collapses precision. In the failure-prototype region, MetroPT fails earlier: no exceedance is produced at all, so the held-out failure never enters the learned region. The SPOT-style baseline fails differently again, raising the fewest alarms of any method and matching none of them. Non-event datasets add stress-test evidence only; their cycles, vehicles, and wafers are not independent compressor failure episodes.

Target-region methods add diagnostic value by exposing heterogeneity between MetroPT and MetroPT2, but no evaluated method establishes reliable operational early warning. The evidence required next is specific: more independent failure episodes, prospectively frozen target regions, stable regime definitions, deployment-realistic alarm policies, and external validation showing acceptable recall, false-alarm burden, lead time, and utility under the same event-level protocol.
