\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Estimator finite-sample validity}

The Monte Carlo results support a bounded interpretation of the EVT diagnostics. Runs, Ferro--Segers, K-gaps, reciprocal mean cluster size, block clustering, and no-declustering references are useful for detecting estimator instability across process families, thresholds, missingness, and dynamical structure. They do not by themselves identify maintenance-relevant degradation. The practical implication is that recurrence estimates should be treated as conditional diagnostics tied to the chosen observable, threshold, run length, and sampling process.

\subsection{Target-region transferability}

The central empirical finding is target-region heterogeneity. The failure-prototype region missed the held-out MetroPT failure, yet detected the held-out MetroPT2 failure with 157 alarm episodes and 9.41 false alarms per operating day. That result is materially better than the registered recurrence score on MetroPT2, but it does not transfer back to MetroPT. The evidence therefore supports target-region instability, not a general target-region solution.

\subsection{Score, Threshold, and Alarm Failure}

The failure mode is layered. Some methods fail at the score layer because failure-proximal states are not separated from normal states. Others produce score information that is lost or made operationally unusable by threshold choice. The registered recurrence score detects both compressor failures, but alarm conversion leaves hundreds or thousands of episodes. That is an alarm-policy failure after thresholding, not a calibrated maintenance signal.

\subsection{Comparison With Baselines}

Simple baselines remain competitive under the held-out event policy. The engineering threshold detected both compressor events and reduced alarm burden relative to the registered score. Classical EVT and anomaly baselines did not remove the basic precision problem. The proposed recurrence score therefore adds diagnostic structure, but it does not add measurable operational value on these held-out events.

\subsection{Matched Negative Controls}

Matched controls prevent over-interpreting one favorable target-region result. For MetroPT2, the failure-prototype region has lower false-alarm burden than rare-region and phase-randomised controls, but occupancy-matched random regions can have lower burden. The result is promising as a diagnostic contrast, not as evidence that the learned region is uniquely failure-proximal.

\subsection{Independent-Event Scarcity}

Only two independent held-out compressor failures are available. This prevents stable estimation of event-level uncertainty, calibration, and failure-type heterogeneity. Timestamp counts cannot substitute for independent failures because alarm episodes are temporally dependent and can cluster around the same operating regime.

\subsection{Operational Implications}

The workflow is useful as an audit of how extreme-score methods fail. It exposes whether apparent score excursions survive thresholding, declustering, alarm merging, and event matching. It does not establish deployable early warning. Deployment would require prospectively validated target regions, enough independent failures or entities, acceptable false-alarm burden, useful lead time, and comparison against simple policies under the same episode-level evaluation.
