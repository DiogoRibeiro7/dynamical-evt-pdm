\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The main scientific lesson is negative. Clustered extremes and low extremal-index diagnostics can be observable in industrial telemetry, but that does not imply useful early warning. The conversion from pointwise exceedances to alarm episodes is a separate modelling decision. In the registered threshold grid, that conversion produced a failed-detection region; in the compressor diagnostics, event recall was possible only with thousands of predicted alarm episodes and very low precision.

The likely mechanisms differ by dataset role. For MetroPT and MetroPT2, the limiting factors are sparse independent failures, target-region uncertainty, and alarm burden. A single detected held-out event cannot support a stable estimate of event-level performance when the alarm policy generates many episodes. For SCANIA Component X, the issue is not merely performance; it is a different estimand, because vehicle-level repair-risk ranking is not compressor early warning. Hydraulic Systems and SECOM show another boundary: condition-state and yield labels can be processed by the software, but they do not supply event-onset evidence for dynamical-EVT alarm claims.

The extremal index should therefore be read as a dependence diagnostic. It can identify clustering under a chosen observable, threshold, and run length, but it is not a mechanical degradation variable. Regime mixture, smoothing, missingness, periodic control behavior, and persistent shifts can all create clustered exceedances. Useful deployment would require showing that the target region is identifiable from training data, that failures recur through that region before onset, that enough independent failures or entities exist for uncertainty estimation, and that the alarm policy remains useful after episode conversion.

Calibration is similarly limited. The current outputs are ranks or anomaly scores. Reliability-style plots are useful for detecting score-orientation mistakes and split drift, but they are not evidence of calibrated failure probabilities unless a separate calibration partition contains enough independent positive units and the score-to-probability map is tested on held-out data.

The reproducibility layer remains important because it prevents negative results from being silently edited away. The repository checks generated references, citations, unsupported claim language, compiled PDFs, and artifact manifests. These checks do not make the method scientifically successful. They make the boundary between supported evidence and unsupported interpretation explicit.
