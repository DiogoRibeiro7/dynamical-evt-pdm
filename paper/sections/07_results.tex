\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The generated results support a diagnostic and reproducibility claim, not an industrial superiority claim. Each numerical table and figure used below is produced by version-controlled code under experiment identifier \DynEvtExperimentId{} and dataset checksum \DynEvtDatasetChecksum{}.

Threshold and run-length sensitivity is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:threshold-stability} and Table~\ref{tab:threshold-stability}. The grid changes exceedance and cluster behavior, supporting CLM-002. This is expected under dependent telemetry and is the reason the manuscript reports sensitivity rather than a single chosen threshold as if it were model-free.

The alarm frontier in Figure~\ref{fig:frontier} and Table~\ref{tab:frontier} reports the trade-off between event recall, lead time, and false-alarm event rate. It supports CLM-004 only as an exploratory operational trade-off. In particular, a visually acceptable frontier is not evidence of calibrated failure probability, and a reduction in duplicate alarms would be useful only if recall, lead time, and time under warning remain acceptable.

Calibration is reported as a reliability diagnostic rather than a probability guarantee. Figure~\ref{fig:reliability} uses ranked horizon-risk surrogates and observed future-event indicators. This supports CLM-005: calibration claims require frozen out-of-sample probability calibration and uncertainty bands, which are not replaced by this diagnostic plot.

\begin{figure}[t]

  \caption{Reliability diagnostic for ranked horizon risk. Bin counts are preserved in the generated table and provenance file.}
  \label{fig:reliability}
\end{figure}

The ablation registry is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}. It supports CLM-006 as a coverage claim: the artifact records the registered ablation families, but families with missing or non-estimable effect summaries still require direct artifact inspection before a stronger statement is made.
