\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The generated results support a diagnostic and reproducibility claim, not a broad industrial performance claim. Each numerical table and figure used below is produced by version-controlled code under experiment identifier \DynEvtExperimentId{} and dataset checksum \DynEvtDatasetChecksum{}.

Threshold and run-length sensitivity is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:threshold-stability} and Table~\ref{tab:threshold-stability}. The grid changes exceedance and cluster behavior, supporting CLM-002. This is expected under dependent telemetry and is the reason the manuscript reports sensitivity rather than a single chosen threshold as if it were model-free.

The alarm frontier in Figure~\ref{fig:frontier} and Table~\ref{tab:frontier} reports the trade-off between event recall, lead time, and false-alarm event rate. It supports CLM-004 only as an exploratory operational trade-off. In particular, a visually acceptable frontier is not evidence of calibrated failure probability, and a reduction in duplicate alarms would be useful only if recall, lead time, and time under warning remain acceptable.

  \caption{Lead-time versus false-alarm frontier generated under the frozen event-matching protocol.}

\end{figure}

Matching sensitivity is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:matching-tolerance} and Table~\ref{tab:matching-tolerance}. This surface varies both the alarm merge gap and the post-onset matching tolerance, making explicit whether an operational conclusion depends on one favorable episode-conversion rule. It extends CLM-004 but does not change its permitted strength: the evidence is still an exploratory operational trade-off.

Calibration is reported as a reliability diagnostic rather than a probability guarantee. Figure~\ref{fig:reliability} uses ranked horizon-risk surrogates and observed future-event indicators. Figure~\ref{fig:split-calibration} and Table~\ref{tab:split-calibration} add split-aware intervals using training-ranked risk scores. This supports CLM-005 as a narrow diagnostic claim: generated intervals are now present, but they are not a substitute for a prospective calibrated probability model.
