\begin{abstract}
Does a dynamical-EVT workflow turn clustered extreme scores into useful maintenance alarms on public industrial monitoring data? On this evidence, no. A Monte Carlo study over fourteen process families at 500 replications per cell shows the ranking of six extremal-index estimators inverting between regimes: runs and K-gaps reach interval coverage of \(0.40\) against a nominal \(0.95\) for an independent process, yet are the least biased of the six once extremes genuinely cluster. Nineteen methods are then evaluated on two independent held-out compressor failures under one shared alarm policy. The registered recurrence score matched both, at 2631 and 852 alarm episodes and 105.3 and 51.5 false alarms per operating day, but the two matches differ in kind. Measuring how well each score ranks the hour before onset against the rest of the test split, it separates at \(0.86\) on MetroPT2 but \(0.45\) on MetroPT, below the chance value of \(0.50\), where a detector raising 2631 episodes matches by coincidence with probability \(0.99\). MetroPT is a score-layer failure rather than an alarm-conversion one, as it is for sixteen of the nineteen methods there. A single-sensor threshold beats the recurrence score on both datasets. Target regions transfer only directionally, and only on the five of seven features with cross-dataset distributional support. The best benchmark result, the failure-prototype region on MetroPT2 at 9.4 false alarms per day, does not survive conditioning matched controls on detection, falling from the 65th to the 28th percentile of joint utility against occupancy-matched random regions. No evaluated method is operationally plausible on these events, and two failures cannot establish that any would be.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Code and Data Availability}

Code, configuration files, data-acquisition scripts, generated-paper tooling, and reproducibility materials are maintained in the project repository \citep{Ribeiro2026DynEvtPdmRepository}. It carries a semantic version tag, a BSD-3-Clause licence, \texttt{CITATION.cff} and \texttt{codemeta.json} metadata, release notes, and an artifact manifest recording the hash of every generated figure and table, so every number in this paper is traceable to the commit that produced it.

The repository is public, and the results in this paper correspond to the tagged release \texttt{v0.3.2}. A reader can retrieve that state, install the pinned environment from the lock file, and re-run the pipeline that produced every figure and table reported here.

\textbf{No archival DOI has been minted.} The citation above therefore resolves to a hosted repository, which is mutable and could in principle be moved or deleted, rather than to an immutable deposit. Minting one requires depositing a release snapshot with an archive service, which is an account action outside the build. Until that is done this paper does not claim a persistent software identifier, and the version tag together with the commit hash recorded in the artifact manifest is what fixes the state that produced these results.

Raw third-party datasets are not redistributed by this repository; the acquisition pipeline stores them locally under \texttt{data/raw/} and documents their public sources through the dataset registry.
