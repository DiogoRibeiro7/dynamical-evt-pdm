\title{When Clustered Extremes Do Not Become Early Warnings: A Negative-Result Dynamical-EVT Study for Public Industrial Monitoring Data}
\author{Diogo Ribeiro}
\date{August 3, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
This paper asks whether clustered extreme scores from a dynamical-EVT workflow become useful maintenance alarms on public industrial monitoring data, and answers no. The evidence has two parts. A Monte Carlo study covers fourteen process families at 500 replications per configured cell, with 1000 replications on a preregistered subset, comparing six extremal-index estimators against theoretical references where a closed form exists and numerical ones otherwise. Its central result is that the estimator ranking inverts between regimes: runs and K-gaps record interval coverage of 0.40 and 0.39 against a nominal 0.95 for the independent process, yet are the least biased of the six once extremes genuinely cluster. A reader seeing only near-independent processes would reject precisely the estimators that work. Coverage at \(\theta = 1\) is further distorted by boundary clipping, which affects every estimator and is reported alongside it.

The empirical evidence contains two independent held-out compressor failures, one in MetroPT and one in MetroPT2, and nineteen methods evaluated under one shared alarm policy. The registered recurrence score matched both failures, with 2631 and 852 alarm episodes, 105.34 and 51.47 false alarms per operating day, and event precision below 0.12\%. Those two matches do not mean the same thing. Measuring how well each score ranks the hour before onset against the rest of the test split, the score separates at 0.86 on MetroPT2 but at 0.46 on MetroPT, below the chance value of 0.50; a detector raising 2631 episodes matches the MetroPT failure with probability 0.99 while carrying no signal at all. The MetroPT result is a score-layer failure, not an alarm-conversion one, as it is for fourteen of the nineteen methods on that dataset including the engineering threshold and the classical peaks-over-threshold fit. A single-sensor threshold is the best simple baseline and beats the registered score on both datasets, on precision, on false alarms per day and on event utility; the fixed engineering threshold reaches a lower alarm rate only by holding the system under warning for 75.1\% of the test period against the registered score's 0.12\%, so episode counts and exposure disagree in direction and neither alone establishes an advantage.

Target regions were tested for genuine cross-dataset transfer rather than compared across datasets. Two of seven features have no distributional overlap between the datasets and are excluded, and on the five compatible features transfer is strongly asymmetric: the MetroPT region applied directly to MetroPT2 reaches precision 0.0057 at 10.5 false alarms per day, better than a region fitted on MetroPT2 itself, while the reverse direction occupies 50.3\% of the test period at precision 0.00033. Recalibration rescues the failing direction and degrades the working one, so it cannot be folded into a single transfer number. The same feature restriction explains the region's MetroPT non-detection: five compatible features detect that failure, all seven produce zero alarms.

The strongest target-region result, on MetroPT2, weakens once controls are conditioned on detection, because a region raising no alarms otherwise attains the best possible burden while missing the failure. Against occupancy-matched random regions it falls from the 65th percentile of joint utility to the 28th, and against prototype-source permutations from the 66th to the 27th; it still exceeds every detecting draw in the two families whose draws almost always detect. On MetroPT its zero burden is vacuous: neither it nor any of the 3000 control draws detected the failure. The conclusion is narrow and negative. These diagnostics expose finite-sample estimator instability, directional and feature-dependent transfer, and the layer at which each method fails, but no evaluated method is operationally plausible on these two failures, and two failures cannot establish that any would be.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Code and Data Availability}

Code, configuration files, data-acquisition scripts, generated-paper tooling, and reproducibility materials are maintained in the project repository \citep{Ribeiro2026DynEvtPdmRepository}. The repository carries a semantic version tag, a BSD-3-Clause licence, \texttt{CITATION.cff} and \texttt{codemeta.json} metadata, release notes, and an artifact manifest recording the hash of every generated figure and table, so any number in this paper can be traced to the commit that produced it.

\textbf{The archival DOI is not yet minted.} Depositing the release requires an account action on the archive service, which is outside the build: the repository must be connected to Zenodo and a GitHub release published against the version tag before a DOI is issued. Until that step is completed by the authors, the citation above resolves to a mutable repository rather than an immutable archive, and this paper does not claim a persistent software identifier. The version tag and commit hash in the artifact manifest are the reproducibility anchor in the interim.

Raw third-party datasets are not redistributed by this repository; the acquisition pipeline stores them locally under \texttt{data/raw/} and documents their public sources through the dataset registry.
