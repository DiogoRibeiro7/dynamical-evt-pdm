\title{When Clustered Extremes Do Not Become Early Warnings: A Negative-Result Dynamical-EVT Study for Public Industrial Monitoring Data}
\author{Diogo Ribeiro}
\date{July 27, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This paper evaluates whether clustered extreme scores from a dynamical-EVT-inspired workflow become useful maintenance alarms on public industrial monitoring data. The study combines high-replication Monte Carlo checks across fourteen process families, held-out compressor event evaluation, target-region variants, matched negative controls, and non-event stress tests. The primary empirical evidence contains two independent held-out compressor failures: one in MetroPT and one in MetroPT2. The registered recurrence score detected both, but with 2527 and 849 alarm episodes, false-alarm burdens of 101.17 and 51.29 alarms per operating day, and event precision below 0.12\%. The best simple engineering threshold also detected both events and reduced alarm burden relative to the registered score. The strongest target-region result was heterogeneous: the failure-prototype region missed the held-out MetroPT event entirely, but detected the MetroPT2 event with 157 alarms, 9.41 false alarms per day, and precision \(0.00637\). Matched controls show that this MetroPT2 burden is lower than rare-region and phase-randomised controls, but not uniformly better than occupancy-matched random regions. The narrow conclusion is therefore negative and conditional: recurrence-based EVT diagnostics can expose finite-sample estimator instability, target-region heterogeneity, and alarm-conversion failure, but these public datasets do not establish operationally reliable early warning.
\end{abstract}
