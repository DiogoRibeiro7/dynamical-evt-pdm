\title{When Clustered Extremes Do Not Become Early Warnings: A Negative-Result Dynamical-EVT Study for Public Industrial Monitoring Data}
\author{Diogo Ribeiro}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Industrial early-warning studies often begin with pointwise anomaly or exceedance scores, while maintenance decisions are made at the level of alarm episodes, failures, vehicles, or repair actions. This paper uses a negative-result dynamical-EVT study on public industrial monitoring data to test whether score-level extremes survive that conversion. The evaluated assets combine causal state construction, train-split robust scoring, threshold sensitivity, extremal-index diagnostics, declustered alarm episodes, and event-level matching where event labels are available. The primary event-level case studies are MetroPT and MetroPT2; SCANIA Component X is treated as a vehicle-level stress test, while Hydraulic Systems and SECOM are condition-monitoring demonstrations with different targets. The strongest empirical result is also the central limitation: the registered compressor diagnostics recovered the single labelled test event in both MetroPT and MetroPT2, but only with 2527 and 849 predicted alarm episodes and precision below 0.12\%. A separate threshold-grid alarm analysis produced zero event recall across all registered quantiles. Hydraulic Systems, SCANIA, and SECOM produced \(F_1=0\) under the conservative high-quantile diagnostic. These results support a narrow conclusion: dynamical-EVT-inspired clustering diagnostics can expose dependence and alarm-conversion failure modes, but on the evaluated public data they did not provide reliable operational early warning. Useful deployment requires identifiable failure-proximal recurrence, sufficient independent events or entities, stable regime definitions, and alarm policies evaluated at the episode level.
\end{abstract}
