\title{When Clustered Extremes Do Not Become Early Warnings: A Negative-Result Dynamical-EVT Study for Public Industrial Monitoring Data}
\author{Diogo Ribeiro}
\date{July 28, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This paper evaluates whether clustered extreme scores from a dynamical-EVT-inspired workflow become useful maintenance alarms on public industrial monitoring data. The study combines high-replication Monte Carlo checks across fourteen process families, held-out compressor event evaluation, target-region variants, matched negative controls, and non-event stress tests. The primary empirical evidence contains two independent held-out compressor failures: one in MetroPT and one in MetroPT2. The registered recurrence score matched both, but with 2631 and 852 alarm episodes, false-alarm burdens of 105.34 and 51.47 alarms per operating day, and event precision below 0.12\%. Those matches mean different things on the two datasets. Measuring how well each score ranks the hour before onset against the rest of the test split, the score separates at 0.86 on MetroPT2 but at 0.46 on MetroPT, below the chance value of 0.50; a detector raising 2631 episodes matches the MetroPT failure with probability 0.99 while carrying no signal at all. The MetroPT result is therefore a score-layer failure rather than an alarm-conversion one, as it is for fourteen of the nineteen methods on that dataset, including the engineering threshold and the classical peaks-over-threshold fit. The best simple engineering threshold also detected both events, and whether it reduces alarm burden depends on which burden is measured: it raises fewer alarm episodes on MetroPT (1551 against 2631) while holding the system under warning for 75.1\% of the test period against the registered score's 0.12\%. Episode counts and exposure disagree in direction here, so neither alone establishes an operational advantage. The strongest target-region result was heterogeneous: the failure-prototype region missed the held-out MetroPT event entirely, but detected the MetroPT2 event with 157 alarms, 9.41 false alarms per day, and precision \(0.00637\). Conditioning that comparison on detection weakens it in every control family, and reverses it in two. Against occupancy-matched random regions the MetroPT2 region falls from the 65th percentile of joint utility to the 28th once only detecting controls are admitted, and against prototype-source permutations from the 66th to the 27th; against phase-randomised and regime-matched rare controls it still exceeds every detecting draw. On MetroPT the region's zero alarm burden is vacuous rather than favourable, because neither it nor any of the 3000 control draws detected the failure at all. The narrow conclusion is therefore negative and conditional: recurrence-based EVT diagnostics can expose finite-sample estimator instability, target-region heterogeneity, and alarm-conversion failure, but these public datasets do not establish operationally reliable early warning.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Code and Data Availability}

Code, configuration files, data-acquisition scripts, generated-paper tooling, and reproducibility materials are maintained in the project repository \citep{Ribeiro2026DynEvtPdmRepository}. The repository carries a semantic version tag, a BSD-3-Clause licence, \texttt{CITATION.cff} and \texttt{codemeta.json} metadata, release notes, and an artifact manifest recording the hash of every generated figure and table, so any number in this paper can be traced to the commit that produced it.

\textbf{The archival DOI is not yet minted.} Depositing the release requires an account action on the archive service, which is outside the build: the repository must be connected to Zenodo and a GitHub release published against the version tag before a DOI is issued. Until that step is completed by the authors, the citation above resolves to a mutable repository rather than an immutable archive, and this paper does not claim a persistent software identifier. The version tag and commit hash in the artifact manifest are the reproducibility anchor in the interim.

Raw third-party datasets are not redistributed by this repository; the acquisition pipeline stores them locally under \texttt{data/raw/} and documents their public sources through the dataset registry.
