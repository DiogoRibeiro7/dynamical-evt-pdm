\begin{abstract}
Industrial predictive-maintenance studies often compress heterogeneous monitoring problems into pointwise anomaly scores. That framing loses the distinction between telemetry samples, alarm episodes, repair histories, component-condition cycles, and production-quality outcomes. This paper reports a reproducible dynamical-EVT diagnostic workflow evaluated through a five-dataset evidence matrix spanning MetroPT, MetroPT2, SCANIA Component X, UCI Hydraulic Systems, and UCI SECOM. The implementation combines causal state construction, regime-conditioned thresholds, declustering, extremal-index diagnostics, event-level matching, conservative tabular condition diagnostics, baseline provenance, and a frozen evaluation protocol. The completed asset bundle records \DynEvtClaimCount{} claim-ledger entries, of which \DynEvtSupportedClaimCount{} are permitted for manuscript use under the generated ledger. The present evidence supports a bounded conclusion: the workflow makes dependence, clustering, alarm burden, calibration diagnostics, estimand changes, and weak real-data results visible across several public industrial-monitoring angles. It does not support a population-level superiority claim beyond the registered evidence matrix. All reported claims are linked to experiment identifier \DynEvtExperimentId{}, protocol hash \DynEvtProtocolHash{}, and generated artifacts.
\end{abstract}
