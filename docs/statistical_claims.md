# Statistical claims and prohibited interpretations

## Claims the repository may support

1. An extremal-index estimator recovers known clustering structure under specified simulations.
2. Regime-conditioned thresholds alter the false-alarm burden relative to global thresholds.
3. Episode-level declustering reduces duplicate operational alarms.
4. Particular pre-failure intervals exhibit stronger or weaker extreme clustering than selected normal intervals.
5. A probability model is more or less calibrated for a defined horizon on a stated split.

## Claims requiring stronger evidence

- broad predictive-maintenance performance beyond the evaluated evidence;
- causal degradation detection;
- proof that an extremal index change is generated by a particular mechanical component;
- reliable estimation of rare-failure probabilities from MetroPT alone;
- generalization to unseen machine types based on one compressor dataset.

## Interpretive caution

The inverse extremal index has an asymptotic mean cluster-size interpretation under appropriate conditions. It is not automatically the mean duration of an industrial fault. Cluster size counts extreme observations; duration additionally depends on sampling and gaps.

A low extremal index can arise from periodicity, persistence, mixture of regimes, preprocessing, measurement effects or model misspecification. Mechanical interpretation requires supporting diagnostics.

## Sensitivity Evidence

The current constrained package includes generated evidence for threshold and run-length stability, alarm merge-gap and matching-tolerance surfaces, target-region variants, negative controls, baseline provenance, and bounded simulation sensitivity over noise and missingness.

Additional stress tests, including larger missing-data, smoothing, downsampling, scaling, distance-metric and prospective validation surfaces, would broaden the evidence base. They are required only for claims beyond the current negative-result scope.
